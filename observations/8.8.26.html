Weekly Summary

- The US has called off further strikes on Iran (as allies have been pressuring them). Additionally, Iran may open the Strait soon! They've been negotiating with the Omani delegation, and negotiators say that they will send a fleet including oil tankers to make sure the Strait is free of mines before allowing other ships to transverse. During this transitional period, Iran will not charge ships a fee, however, in the future they expect to do so. In return, they expect the US to lift sanctions on Iranian oil.

Update: Top officials involved in the deal have been taking a hardline stance on the US, demanding that they repay Iran for war damages as a prerequisite for reopening the Strait. Both delegations continue to distrust each other, especially since the fragile truce from the MoU has basically fallen apart. This continues to pressure Trump as he faces elevated oil prices and inflation ahead of the midterm elections.

- Even though the US unemployment rate fell from 4.2% to 4.1% in June, this statistic was caused by people leaving the labour force, causing the labour force participation rate to fall to a 5 year low (Mutikani). Coupled with non-farm payrolls unexpectedly falling 23,000 in July, this has caused analysts to lower expectations of rate hikes in the next Fed Meeting (taking place in September), with Fed Fund Futures now predicting a 45.9% of a rate hike occurring (CME Group; Mutikani). Lower than expected interest rates benefit capital intensive industries (particularly AI tech stocks), causing the entire market to rebound slightly.

- S&P500 companies have some of the highest earnings growth this quarter, despite facing rising energy costs, potentially higher interest rates, and cautious consumer spending. Multiple sectors are growing at record rates since the pandemic, and systemically, wealth inequality has consistently risen between those who own assets and those who don't. So it's important to get in the market!

- According to tests conducted by UK's AI Security Institute, Anthropic and OpenAI models went rogue in tests, conducting cyberattacks on third-party websites and creating fake profiles to socially engineer developers. This raises concerns about the safety of AI models, which could potentially add to regulatory costs for companies, further worsening their outlook in the near term.

My outlook on AI: Increased CAPEX investments, rising energy (and datacentre) costs, and intense competition to lead this AI race has significantly reduced the free-cash-flow (FCF) of a company. FCF (in non-technical terms), is the amount of money a company can distribute to investors, and is monitored closely by analysts. Thus, by it shrinking across the board, coupled with increased debt issuance / reduced share buybacks, has raised concerns from investors over the sustainability of AI investments. In the short-run (perhaps within the next 3 years), we may see lower AI valuations if those investments continue to fail to generate quality profits for companies. However, in the long-run (which may take 20 years to realise), productivity benefits from AI will eventually be reflected in terms of higher profit margins.

For the global economy, as we face an aging population and geopolitical pressure to become self-reliant, the economy will shift to become more capital intensive, increasing capital's share of the economy. Thus, long-term investments in capital and pouring money into AI isn't necessarily a bad idea, it's just that we need to do it consistently and have a long-term investment horizon.

Updates on Individual Stocks

- Due to leasing more data centres to AI companies, SpaceX's revenues and losses are better than expectations, but its stock price fell as Musk announced that it will increase CAPEX on AI investments. In face of this result, Musk has argued that SpaceX's internet division, Starlink, is undervalued, and the potential of building datacentres in space justifies its expensive IPO valuation. On a side note, analysts believe that Musk will try to merge SpaceX with Tesla, consolidating his various businesses.
