US Inflation Outlook

As of August, the annual US inflation rate remained at 3.4%, with core inflation dropping by 0.1 percentage points to 2.4% (Trading Economics). This seems good right? Yes, but that's not the full picture. Because the market is forward-looking, investors focus on leading indicators (statistics which tell you about the economy in the future; in our case, statistics that tell us how inflation will look like), rather than lagging indicators like CPI and core CPI.

Instead, news reports mention that US manufacturers face cost-push inflation as the prices of raw materials and energy have increased. The Producer Price Index (PPI), a LEADING INDICATOR, has increased by 0.4% month-on-month. As producers ultimately pass on prices to consumers, this suggests a period of higher inflation for households, and prices for products made with petroleum, steel, and aluminium have increased (McCormick). This is also why the Federal Reserve implemented a 25 basis point rate hike on Wednesday.

Will that 25 basis point rate hike be enough to control inflation? Personally, I don't think so. According to Fed Fund Futures, the market still prices in 1-2 rate hikes by the end of the year (CME Group). Moreover, as trade talks between the US and Canada break down, Canada's Prime Minister has requested the EU to deepen trade relations as they seek to reduce reliance on the US. They have sought to get "associate" membership with the EU. The problem with this is that if they do so, Trump threatens to implement steeper tariffs or embargoes on goods and services, which ultimately hurt the consumer, leading to even higher prices.

Higher inflation hurts fixed income, leading to higher bond yields. This then further exacerbates concerns over the sustainability of US debt, pushing long-term bond yields to record highs, undermining the risk-free status of US treasuries. As now more money is flowing into US stocks than debt, analysts suggest that the value of the US dollar may be more linked to stocks than bonds.

Now, the US has a few options. Firstly, they can grow their way out of debt (e.g. through the AI industry), which would ease investor concerns and lower bond yields. That's the bullish case. If not, they may have to inflate away the real value of debt (which is probably what Trump wants), which will increase the cost of living for households. Subsequently, inequality is likely to increase, and thus explains the rationale for buying more real assets.

Geopolitics

Iran's proxy, the Houthis (which are situated in Yemen), have been targetting the Bab al-Mandeb Strait (the other side of Saudi Arabia, which is currently used for oil exports since the Strait of Hormuz remains blocked). Subsequently, Saudi Arabia shut down the east-west pipeline due to drone attacks, causing the price of oil to increase to $109 USD a barrel. Their exports fell to 6.2 million barrels in August (England).

In Europe, the EU has asked China to restrict their hybrid car exports, in order to reduce the EU's trade deficit. Such dumping activities have caused massive layoffs for domestic car manufacturers, and to counteract this, they may implement higher tariffs.

On an unrelated note, Nigerian company Dangote Petroleum Refinery, which refines 700,000 barrels a day, is aiming to raise $1.6 billion USD in its IPO on the Nigerian Stock Exchange (NGX). Online brokers have increased liquidity and retail participation, and the share price of $0.40 USD a share has been seen as attractive to the local population.

Technology

Due to concerns that AI will destroy humanity, executives and engineers from Anthropic, Google and OpenAI have called to coordinate and slow down AI development. However, Nivida, Meta, and Trump do not agree. It's easier to understand Nvidia and Trump's stance. To Nvidia, since they sell the chips (the "shovels") AI requires to function, obviously they wouldn't want AI development to slow down, or they'll lose revenue. Trump, who is dealing with inflation and debt sustainability, may want the US economy to grow out of debt, as aforementioned in the first section.

OpenAI plans to undergo another private funding round at a $1.2 trillion USD valuation. They plan on capitalising their GPT-5.6 and Astra models before going public. According to the FT, they are unlikely to be listed before 2027 due to AI safety and regulatory concerns (Steer and Hammond).
