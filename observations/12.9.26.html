Another Gold Rush?

As Trump's capricious policies create macroeconomic uncertainty, from waging trade wars with Canada (the US has recently banned imports of dairy products, motorcycles and alcohol as retaliation*), to re-starting the war with Iran, gold has retained its value as a "safe haven" asset. In recent months, gold has become the largest holding within central bank reserves, overtaking US treasuries, and countries are increasingly repatriating their gold away from the US, potentially back to their home country. This week, the Dutch Central Bank moved 78 tonnes of their gold bullion from New York to London, and the French Central Bank moved all their gold in January 2026 (Stonor and Wallace).

This fear of the US financial system stems from the Russian-Ukraine war, where the US froze $300 billion in Russian central bank assets and restricted gold-related transactions with Russia's central bank. Since then, sanctioned Russian gold exports have been flowing to China through HK, strengthening a relationship where Russia sells resources to china for economic support. Other countries have also taken a more cautious approach, shifting gold reserves and becoming less reliant on the dollar.

If this trend continues, what we may see is the dollar's dominance weakening over the long-term, and the price of gold (as the universal and most traditional form of money) increasing. Gold's price increases are largely attributed to less confidence in the US controlling their debt situation (captured by the term premium). However, according to analysts, its positive correlation to bonds and equity reduces its diversification benefits, making it lose some of its purpose in acting as a safe haven during a market downturn.

*If you think that these embargoes increase inflation, you are most certainly right. Not to worry though, as President Trump will give every American voter $5000 USD if Republicans retain control over congress in November's midterm elections! (Erickson et al.)

FICC

Oil rises to $100 a barrel on Monday due to supply chain concerns. The US-Iran war has escalted as Iran recently attacked Saudi Aramco's oil facilities in Saudi Arabia. Moreover, oil reserves outside China have fallen, and transportation remains an issue as there are not enough oil tankers at sea. Refined crude product premiums (e.g. diesel premiums) have also reached all time highs, which incentivises producers to refine their crude instead of selling crude oil directly. This causes traders to take a view that 1) the war will last longer (Trump has already proven that point by claiming that oil prices are unlikely to go down until after the midterm elections), and 2) prices remain elevated over the long-term.

On an unrelated note, the Yen increased on Monday against the dollar, partially due to speculation that Japanese authorities would support the currency. A rate hike by the Bank of Japan (BoJ) is largely priced in (the market thinks there is an 80% chance of a 25 basis point rate hike), however, it is possible that pension funds may purchase higher yielding domestic assets, reducing global Yen supply and sustaining the upward pressure, causing the Yen to appreciate in the short-term (Yamaguchi and Kihara).

Moreover, US bond yields are back to their three-year highs as Bessent's $6 billion USD buyback plan was not enough to calm investors down (Aratani). Although it was higher than the original $4 billion USD minimum announced a few weeks earlier, it was still below analysts expectations of $8-10 billion USD. The market currently prices in a 25 basis point rate hike, which increases bond yields, particularly at the near term of the yield curve.

Tech and Wealth

Meta recently launched a new AI agent called Muse, which cam connect to Meta's social media platforms and help the user send emails, plan dates, or buy goods. Meta claims that user data will not be shared by their advertising systems, and can be deleted by the user.

The stablecoin company Tether launches a $400 million USD private credit fund, known as StableFund, to expand their use of USDT (Asgari). They aim on investing in small and medium-sized businesses who may not have access to regular sources of capital (for example, bank loans, bond markets, or venture capital). Tether faces concerns over USDT adoption slowing (the amount in circulation has been flat at $183 billion USD this year), and USDT being used for illicit activities.

Finally, wealthy investors are buying into investments which will get access to DeepSeek's fundraising, often paying high premiums in order to do so. Why are they rushing? Is it because of FOMO? Or do they think that public markets will return less over the long-run, justifying their purchase into these riskier investments with lower liquidity? Or potentially because the demographic of wealthy clients has shifted due to the intergenerational wealth transfer, and the younger generation has a higher "risk tolerance" (or gambling addiction)?

Regardless, personally, I think there's no need to jump at every opportunity that is presented, especially when the terms are unfavourable. AI will probably be the most transformative technological advancement of the decade, but just like the dot-com bubble, it may take several years for companies to be profitable, and market downturns are inevitable. Additionally, we don't have much of an indication of who will win the AI race, and if companies will be subject to increasing regulatory and national security concerns. I think it's better to buy "value" companies which may experience margin growth from AI adoption, to ride the AI wave without taking as many risks associated. After all, a company's intrinsic value is based on the amount of actual cash flows it generates to investors, not how much debt it can take on or how flashy its new AI models are.
