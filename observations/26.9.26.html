Oil, inflation, and bond yields

Observe the following:

  • On Wednesday, Trump threatened to cut supply to the EU, causing diesel (a product you get from refining crude oil) prices to skyrocket by 7%. It hasn't gone up further as some analysts remain optimistic, believing that the IEA will release strategic reserves to reduce market volatility.
  • On Friday, Iran offers the US a 7-day proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz (again!) and discuss further developments to end the war (Hauslohner). They've based the requirements according to the MOU signed in June, however, the US has previously disagreed to return to those terms. The price of oil fell a little on the announcement, but has still remained above $100 a barrel.
  • From their summit, Trump and Xi have agreed to extend their trade truce until January 2027, but analysts expected more to be accomplished (Lewis).

    • Our short-term macroeconomic outlook is uncertain; we may expect a sideways market. Obviously, there is some good news and upside potential (from 2) where oil pressures ease), but that may be offset by 1), and given that Iran is proposing the deal, it suggests they're desperate, so Trump might want to hit them harder to put a more permanent end to their nuclear program, in which case oil prices will increase even further into 2027. Alternatively, if 1) were implemented, it would be deterimental to US consumers as well, as international disel prices exacerbate domestic inflation.

    This uncertainty and potentially higher inflation in the long-run has caused long-term bond yields to soar worldwide. Despite this, however, emerging markets (EM) have been selling more foreign currency bonds (debt issued in a currency other than the home country's currency), and the spread between US treasuries and emerging market bonds has fallen to 220 basis points. From this narrowing spread, we can see that US treasuries are potentially losing its risk-free status, because of the uncertainty in the future of the US economy and their debt sustainability. Moreover, EM countries are choosing to issue in Euros or RMB instead of the dollar due to having lower interest rates or diversification benefits (to reduce currency risk).

    Private Corporations

    OpenAI has hacked into an Australian health service website, Medicare Statistics Reporting Service, and accessed internal documents in June. This further exacerbates concerns that AI has been getting out of control. However, if AI companies collectively agree to slow the growth of AI development, this will significantly impact growth and hurt stock market valuations.

    On an unrelated note, McDonald's hopes to gain 1.5 percentage points of market share in chicken globally by 2030, in order to counteract sluggish demand for its burgers. Chicken demand has increased in recent years because of it costing less than beef (and other substitutes), a good source of protein, and having l ess concerns compared to other meats. McDonald's also wants to improve menus, service, and marketing.
