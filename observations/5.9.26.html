To hike or not to hike?

Let's start with geopolitics. US-Iran tensions have remained unstable. The US struck 2 Iranian rocket launchers, and in retaliation, Iran fired several missles at US bases in Jordan. Prior to the attack, the US continued implementing economic sanctions and blocking the Strait of Hormuz (preventing Iranian oil exports) in order to pressure Iran. However, the US treasury is reluctant to implement secondary sanctions on institutions that finance the Iranian regime over fear that it will further destabilise the financial markets. As the war continues to escalate, the US 10-year bond yield increased by 0.05 percentage points to 4.79% on Wednesday; oil prices have correspondingly risen (Trading Economics).

However, Trump seems unfazed. After Venezuelan politicians passed a law allowing private companies to operate oil wells completely, he claims that the US will take control of 65 billion barrels of oil as part of a joint venture. Chevron, in particular, seems particularly keen, and plans on using $7 billion USD to double Venezuela's oil output (Dang and Parraga). Through joint ventures with Venezuela's state oil company, they aim to develop 2 oil fields within the oil-rich Orinoco belt.

Obviously, this unlocks new reserves for America, which may be used to tame global oil markets and supposedly, inflation. But whether or not the average American can truly benefit from this depends on whether the oil is kept within the US or exported overseas. Personally (please don't take my word for granted), I think that it's probably going to be exported, which, by the law of supply, should ease some pressure on global oil prices, but not by much. Thus, Americans may still continue to face higher prices than expected, and its impact on domestic inflation may be limited. On the bright side, at least oil companies may benefit from higher profits.

Now looking at interest rates, Chris Waller says there are recent signs that inflation has come under control, so he supports holding rates at 3.5-3.75% in next meeting (Cox). Yet, Warsh's Jackson Hole speech signalled that persistent inflation should be combatted with additional rate hikes. Non-farm payrolls were 162,000 in August, beating market expectations, giving the FED a buffer if they need to increase rates. Markets are quite split, with only a 58.4% chance of a rate hike is implied from Fed Fund Futures (CMEGroup). Based on this macroeconomic environment, I think a 25 basis point rate hike is likely in the next meeting (again, this is not financial advice), which may increase bond yields further. Stock valuations may be revised lower as the discount rate increases, and gold may fall slightly (as the opportunity cost for holding a non-yielding asset increases). Regardless, given the split decision, markets may remain volatile in the coming two weeks.

Technology and AI

Big Tech companies have been using gains from their AI equity investments to inflate their profits, making it difficult to tell how much is from organic (i.e. internal) growth. They have been booking a larger and larger proportion of their income in the "other income" section of financial statements.

Moreover, Nvidia has agreed to acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 billion USD (Velasquez). Hugging Face is an AI company that develops "open" AI models - the ones that users can download and run on their own laptops. Currently, they have hosted 3 million "open" AI models and 500,000 datasets. Why have they done this? A potential reason is to diversify which company leads the AI ecosystem. Only through intense competition between AI companies in this modern day gold rush can Nvidia continue to sell its shovels.

On an unrelated note, OpenAI released Astra, an AI model which it claims is better than Anthropic's, and is a form of Artificial General Inteligence (AGI), where AI tools have superior performance to humans across a range of tasks. However, whether Astra can increase profits for OpenAI is another question; perhaps we will see a similar situation to last week, where companies, despite developing advanced AI models, are failing to successfully commercialise it. Google has also announced good news: they don't need to divest its digital advertising arm and does not need to sell AdX, as ruled by a judge who dismissed the DoJ.
