Time for real assets?

US-Canada trade talks broke down as Canada believes that the deal (which was supposed to be signed last Friday) was unfair. Canada claimed they will match US tariffs dollar-for-dollar, which puts pressure on Trump as he faces midterm elections admist an inflationary environment. However, instead of negotiating, Trump claims to increase tariffs on Canadian cars and automotive parts to 50% from the original 25% (Yusif). As the car manufacturing process in the US involves moving parts and assembling across both sides of the border, analysts believe that any implementation of tariffs will increase the price of automotives in the US, furthering inflation.

This has obviously made Canadians mad, and they have retaliated by implmenting 50% tariffs on $20 billion USD worth of goods, causing a trade war. Trump's spiteful remarks of renaming Lake Ontario as Lake America has further evoked anger from Canadians, who mostly support their president's protectionist policies against Trump even though it causes domestic inflation.

Let's analyse this from the US's side. Canada, being its second largest trade partner, has a huge effect on prices, and this policy would likely cause more inflation, which would make the FED more likely to implement hike rates. However, if they do increase interest rates, that raises the cost of borrowing for themselves too, causing bond yields to rise and worsening the debt burden (which the US Treasury is trying their best to reduce). If investors find US debt unsustainable, investors would price that into its currency, potentially causing the dollar to depreciate, and making it more difficult for US consumers to buy imported goods, which again, causes more inflation. So it's a bit of a conundrum.

Now suppose the FED doesn't hike rates. Then in which case inflation will keep rising; petrol has reached 5 year highs and mortage prices have also increased, creating an affordability crisis for consumers. The US dollar may depreciate as investors demand a higher inflation premium. Moreover, this likely affects the nominal returns of fixed income, leading to higher yields, which would also worsen the debt problem...

In both cases, the US is likely to lose. If we assume TACO doesn't apply (i.e. Trump doesn't chicken out), then we can consider the following:

1. The US dollar will likely extend its depreciation in the short-run. In that case, currencies that are less exposed to US trade tensions (e.g. GBP, AUD, CNY), are likely to rise. Bonds are likely to extend their sell-off as fixed income loses its charm. Geopolitical uncertainty has caused investors to increase inflows into crypto, allowing Bitcoin to regain its $80,000 USD mark and Ethereum to reclaim its $2500 mark. However, they still have a long way to go before I'm in the green :(

2. We're in an inflationary environment where it's uncertain whether the FED will hike rates or not. If they do hike rates, bonds yields increase, and stock prices decrease as the discount rate becomes higher, so its bad for both asset classes. This may provide an opportunity for real assets (in particular, a shiny yellow metal) which are less correlated with the US economy, and traditionally provide a hedge against inflation.

India's dependence on Russian oil

India has become ever more reliant on imported Russian oil since the US-Iran war. In response to this, they're thinking of building oil reserves, and are terminally diversifying such that their reserves are stored in other countries (such as the UAE). However, this may be costly to implement, transport, and replenish.

Moreover, there is a decrease in Indian households that use LPG, as they transition to using natural gas. Yet, both products are refined from crude oil, so the need to transition to alternative energy sources or renewables remains.

Updates on AI companies

Anthropic's Fable 5 revenue underperformed analyst's expectations as it was too expensive for businesses (Hammond). Even though it is currently one of the most advanced AI models out there, most businesses do not need the best model for daily operations. This introduces another dimension to AI rivalry: price competition, which may hamper AI company's profits.

SpaceX will build a $100 billion USD launch site in Louisiana to expand operations and achieve its vision of building data centres in space. Their stock fell on the day of the announcement. Nvidia's stock price soared as it beat earnings on Wednesday and forecasts an increase in sales of 70% next year due to strong customer demand (Rooprai et al.). However, their operating margin is unlikely to improve as they are facing shortages with raw materials, and they still are unable to fully access Chinese markets due to export restrictions by the US. Their profits were inflated by equity investments including shares in SpaceX.

Meta faces a loss of $18 billion USD as it agrees to settle a case with US states over failures to protect children from getting addicted to social media. Having resolved the dispute, its shares rose the day after the announcement.
